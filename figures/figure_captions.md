# Figure captions (Deliverable C)

One caption per figure: what it shows, and the takeaway. Every figure is generated by `notebooks/03_visualizations.ipynb` at 150 dpi.

## fig01_missingness.png

Share of rows that are blank or carry the -999 sentinel, per column, for the plot, weather and price exports. Problems concentrate in the plot table - fertilizer_kg_per_ha is blank in 5.0% of train rows and labor_days_per_ha carries -999 in 3.2% - plus 1 affected price columns; so every one of those columns needed an explicit fix before modeling.

## fig02_before_after_cleaning.png

Raw versus cleaned distributions. Capping at the train 99.5% percentile trims 76 farm-size and 72 fertilizer outliers, 449 blank rainfall values are filled, and 4 prices recorded in birr per kg are multiplied by 100, so the price histogram no longer shows a spike near zero - without that fix revenue would have been understated 100-fold.

## fig03_yield_distribution.png

Yield distribution overall (left, mean 2.76, median 2.59 t/ha) and by crop (right). The pooled distribution hides a clear crop ranking: the highest-yielding crop centers near 3.90 t/ha against 2.00 t/ha for the lowest, so crop_type must be in any sensible model.

## fig04_region_crop_heatmap.png

Annotated heatmap of mean yield for every region x crop cell with the number of plots. Cells run from 0.83 t/ha (Somali/teff) to 4.80 t/ha (SNNPR/maize) - a 3.97 t/ha spread - and every cell has at least 534 plots, so the pattern is not small-sample noise.

## fig05_correlation_heatmap.png

Correlation matrix of yield with all numeric plot features and every weather-derived feature. Yield correlates most with wx_extreme_heat_days (-0.46) and wx_season_mean_temp_c (-0.45); among the joined weather features, wx_extreme_heat_days is the strongest at -0.46, and the weather columns are mostly uncorrelated with the plot rainfall column - they carry different information.

## fig06_climate_by_region.png

Monthly mean temperature and rainfall per region, with the June-planting growing season (Jun-Sep) shaded. The regions separate clearly - Somali is hottest at 30.2 degC in Somali and the wettest month overall is Aug at 217 mm - which is why season weather is joined per plot window instead of using one annual number.

## fig07_yield_vs_season_temp.png

Yield against the joined growing-season mean temperature, one panel per crop, with binned means (bins holding at least 20 plots). Each crop peaks in a different band - sweet spots run from 16.0 degC (barley) to 20.1 degC (maize) - so temperature is informative only interacted with crop_type, exactly what a tree model learns natively.

## fig08_price_trends.png

Average price per quintal by crop and year after the unit fix (four rows recorded in birr per kg were multiplied by 100). All five crops rise over the period, fastest is sorghum at +29.9% and slowest teff at +20.0%, with teff remaining the most expensive crop at 8,262 birr/quintal - the level that drives the B1.1 revenue ranking.

## fig09_revenue_by_crop_region.png

Revenue per hectare (yield x 10 quintals x price) by crop and region. The best cell is teff/Oromia at 175,868 birr/ha and the worst barley/Somali at 50,799 birr/ha, a 3.5x difference - crop choice moves revenue far more than region does, which is why the demo reports revenue next to yield.

## fig10_model_comparison.png

Holdout RMSE for all 5 models on the same 80/20 split (seed 42). The mean predictor scores 1.413 t/ha and Ridge halves it to 0.881, while the best model (mlp_neural_net) reaches 0.483 with 5-fold CV 0.481 +/- 0.005 and 0.478 after tuning - trees and the network all beat the baseline by about 66%.

## fig11_predicted_vs_actual_residuals.png

Left: predictions against actual yield for the 3,018 held-out plots with the y = x line. Right: residuals against predictions, centered near zero (RMSE 0.478 t/ha). Errors grow at high yields - the model under-predicts the best plots (91% of plots above 6 t/ha are under-predicted) - which is the bias we look at in D7.

## fig12_feature_importance.png

Permutation importance of the top 12 features; shuffling the best feature crop_type raises RMSE by 1.32 t/ha. 3 of the top 12 are weather-derived (wx_temp_anomaly_c, wx_season_mean_temp_c, wx_extreme_heat_days), so the weather join earns its place: without it the ablation in D5 loses -6.6% of accuracy. crop_type and altitude dominate because yield potential is set first by the crop and then by elevation.
